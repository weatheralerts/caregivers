Weather affects older people differently

Hot nights, icy pavements and sudden storms are uncomfortable for everyone, but they can be genuinely risky for older people. Heat can be hard on the heart and on people taking some medicines; ice increases the risk of falls; power cuts and storm damage can leave someone isolated. Many of these situations can be prepared for, if someone knows in time.

Weather Alerts is an Android app that does the watching. You save a location, choose the weather conditions that matter, set thresholds and decide how far ahead you want to know. It is described on alerts-weather.com as a way to stop constantly checking the forecast: the app notifies you when conditions reach your chosen levels.

Weather Alerts provides weather information, not medical or emergency monitoring. It does not track a person's wellbeing and is no replacement for care services or emergency numbers.

Alerts that support caring

Heat and heat index

Set temperature and heat index thresholds for a relative's home. A notification a few days ahead gives time to arrange fans, check in more often or plan a visit.

Temperature spikes

Sudden rises in temperature are tracked too, useful in spring and autumn when hot days catch people off guard.

Cold and freezing conditions

Know when freezing conditions are likely, a prompt to talk about heating, warm clothing and avoiding icy steps.

Snow and freezing rain

Rain and snow alerts include sleet and freezing rain, with intensity thresholds.

Storms and official warnings

Supported national services may issue warnings such as severe thunderstorm, winter storm or blizzard warnings, depending on region, shown with agency attribution.

Air quality and UV

AQI, PM2.5 and UV index thresholds help on polluted or very sunny days.

Getting set up together

  1. Add your loved one's address. Save their home as a location, plus your own. Add a day-centre or a second relative's home too.
  2. Choose gentle but useful thresholds. Pick heat index, cold, freezing and storm conditions. Start with a few alerts and adjust over time.
  3. Use the lead time. Be notified immediately or up to seven days before, enough to plan shopping, prescriptions or a visit around the weather.

If the older person uses an Android phone or tablet themselves, the app can be installed for them too. Home-screen widgets show current conditions and active alerts without navigating menus.

Conversation starters, not alarms

Carers often say the hardest part is knowing when to call without seeming to fuss. A weather alert gives a friendly, practical reason: the heatwave starts on Thursday, so shall I bring some cold drinks over? Snow is forecast for Monday, so do you want me to collect your prescription on Sunday? Used this way, alerts become small acts of care rather than worrying notifications.

Caring from a distance

Many carers live in a different town or even a different country. Because each saved location keeps its own alerts, you can follow the weather where your parent lives as closely as the weather where you live. When an alert arrives, it is a natural reason to pick up the phone: Is the house cool enough? Do you have enough food in before the snow? Have the outdoor plant pots been moved before the storm?

Official warnings appear next to custom alerts, so when a severe weather warning is issued for their area, you are not relying on them to have seen the news.

A season of caring alerts

Summer

Heat index and temperature spike rules for a parent's home, plus UV on very sunny days. Notifications a few days ahead help plan cooler routines.

Autumn

Strong gusts and heavy rain alerts are a cue to check gutters, garden furniture and travel plans for appointments.

Winter

Freezing conditions, snow and freezing rain alerts prompt a call about heating, grit for the path and a shopping run before the weather turns.

Spring

Sudden warm spells and changeable weather are flagged by temperature spike alerts, a reminder about lighter clothing and hydration.

You can adjust each rule as the seasons change or as your loved one's needs change. Start small and add alerts only where they genuinely help.

Simple to read, easy to check

The hourly forecast shows temperature, feels-like conditions, precipitation probability, wind and humidity. The daily view looks several days ahead. Widgets put current conditions, forecasts, official warnings and active custom alerts on the home screen. Custom sounds can make weather alerts easy to recognise, and Quiet Hours and Active Hours keep routine notifications from interrupting sleep while critical warnings are still allowed through.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get heat alerts for my parent's town?

Yes. Save their home as a location and set temperature or heat index thresholds for it.

Will I hear about icy conditions where they live?

You can set alerts for freezing conditions and for snow, sleet and freezing rain.

Does the app monitor my relative's health?

No. It provides weather information and notifications only, not health or emergency monitoring.

Can my mother use the app herself?

If she has an Android phone or tablet, she can install it, and widgets show conditions and alerts on the home screen.

Can I follow two homes plus my own?

Yes. Several places can be saved, each with independent alert settings.

Are official storm warnings included?

Official severe weather warnings from supported national services appear, depending on the region, with agency attribution.

Will alerts wake an older person at night?

Quiet Hours can silence routine notifications overnight, with critical warnings still allowed through.
